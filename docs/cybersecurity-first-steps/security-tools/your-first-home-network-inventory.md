# Guide 26: Your First Home Network Inventory

**Estimated Time:** 45–60 minutes

**Difficulty:** Beginner

---

## Have You Ever Wondered...

...how cybersecurity professionals keep track of all the devices on a network?

Before anyone can protect a network, they first need to understand what is connected to it.

That's why creating an inventory is one of the most valuable first steps.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this guide, you should be able to:

- Explain what a network inventory is.
- Identify devices on your home network.
- Document basic information about those devices.
- Understand why inventories are useful.
- Begin thinking like a cybersecurity professional.

---

## Goal

Create your first network inventory using observation and documentation.

---

## Why This Matters

You can't protect what you don't know exists.

One of the first questions professionals ask is:

> "What devices are actually on this network?"

Building an inventory helps answer that question.

It also gives you a useful reference if something changes later.

---

## Plain-English Explanation

Imagine walking into a workshop.

Before organizing tools or making improvements, you'd probably take a look around and see what equipment is already there.

A network inventory is the same idea.

You're simply documenting what exists.

# What Is a Network Inventory?

A network inventory is simply a list of the devices connected to a network.

Examples include:

- Desktop computers
- Laptops
- Phones
- Tablets
- Printers
- Smart TVs
- Streaming devices
- Game consoles
- Security cameras
- Smart speakers
- Network storage
- Routers

---

# What Should You Record?

For now, keep it simple.

Create a table like this.

| Device   | Connection | Approximate Location | Notes             |
| -------- | ---------- | -------------------- | ----------------- |
| Laptop   | Wi-Fi      | Office               | Personal computer |
| Printer  | Wi-Fi      | Office               | Inkjet printer    |
| Smart TV | Wi-Fi      | Living Room          | Streaming apps    |
| Desktop  | Ethernet   | Office               | Main workstation  |

You don't need to record IP addresses yet.

We'll build on this inventory later in your cybersecurity journey.

---

# Observing Your Network

Walk through your home.

Think about every device that communicates over your network.

You may be surprised by what you discover.

Examples include:

- Phones
- Tablets
- Watches
- Smart light bulbs
- Voice assistants
- Streaming sticks
- Smart thermostats
- Security systems
- Garage door openers
- Refrigerators
- Robot vacuums

Modern homes often contain dozens of connected devices.

---

# Why Inventories Matter

Imagine your Wi-Fi suddenly slows down.

If you already know which devices belong on your network, unusual activity becomes much easier to notice.

Inventories also help when:

- Replacing equipment.
- Troubleshooting problems.
- Planning upgrades.
- Improving security.
- Remembering what's connected months later.

---

!!! tip "Learning Tip"

    Good documentation saves time.

    Professionals often spend just as much time documenting systems as they do configuring them.

---

!!! info "Looking Under the Hood"

    Larger organizations often maintain detailed asset inventories containing device names, IP addresses, operating systems, owners, locations, and maintenance history.

    You're creating a simplified version of that same idea.

---

!!! info "Why Professionals Do This"

    Asset inventories help organizations identify unknown devices, remove outdated equipment, and better understand what they're responsible for protecting.

---

## Try It Yourself

Create your own inventory.

Include every network-connected device you can find.

For each device, record:

- Device name
- Wi-Fi or Ethernet
- Approximate location
- Short description

Don't worry if you miss one.

You can always update your inventory later.
