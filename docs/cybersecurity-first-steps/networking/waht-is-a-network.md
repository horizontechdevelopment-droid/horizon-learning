**Estimated Time:** 25–35 minutes

**Difficulty:** Beginner

## Have You Ever Wondered...

...how your phone can send a picture to another phone?

Or how your laptop can load a website that's stored somewhere else in the world?

The answer is simple:

Networks.

## Goal

Understand the basic purpose of computer networks.

---

## Why This Matters

Cybersecurity is largely about protecting communication.

Before we can understand attacks or defenses, we need to understand what is being protected.

That starts with understanding networks.

---

## Plain-English Explanation

A network is simply two or more devices that can communicate with each other.

That's it.

The devices might be:

- Computers
- Phones
- Tablets
- Printers
- TVs
- Cameras
- Game consoles
- Servers

If they can exchange information, they're part of a network.

---

## New Terms

- Network
- Device
- Local Area Network (LAN)
- Internet

---

# Why Networks Exist

Imagine having one computer with no network.

You couldn't:

- Browse websites.
- Watch videos.
- Send email.
- Print to a network printer.
- Play online games.
- Share files.

Networks allow devices to share information.

---

# Local Networks

A network inside your home is called a:

Local Area Network

or

LAN.

Your router usually connects everything together.

---

# The Internet

The Internet is simply an enormous collection of networks connected together.

Your home network connects to your Internet Service Provider.

Your provider connects to many other networks.

Eventually your request reaches the destination you're trying to contact.

---

!!! tip "Learning Tip"

    Don't think of the Internet as one giant computer.

    Think of it as millions of smaller networks connected together.

---

!!! info "Looking Under the Hood"

    Data doesn't travel all at once.

    It is broken into smaller pieces called packets.

    We'll learn more about packets later when we use Wireshark.

---

!!! info "Why Professionals Do This"

    Network professionals often start troubleshooting by asking one question:

    "Can these two devices communicate?"

    That simple question solves many networking problems.
